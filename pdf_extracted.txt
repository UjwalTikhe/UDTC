1. Guiding Principle
Every choice below follows three rules, carried over from the design discussion:
1. Deterministic before ML — the core Positive/Negative/Inconclusive decision must be
explainable in a courtroom, not just a demo.
2. Fail closed — any uncertainty (bad image, no GPS, clock mismatch, missing
signature) degrades to "ﬂagged/inconclusive," never to a silent wrong answer.
3. Zero paid dependency — nothing in this stack requires a credit card, ever. Where a
"free tier" has a catch (rate limits, auto-pause), it's called out explicitly so it doesn't
surprise you during judging.

4. Module-by-Module Design
4.1 Capture & Calibration Engine (Builder 1)
Reference card: print with 4 ArUco markers at corners + a strip of known-value
color/grayscale patches + a unique printed serial + QR code.
Live gating: reject capture (with on-screen reason) if — card not fully in frame,
Laplacian blur variance below threshold, exposure clipped, or reaction timer outside
the reagent's valid reading window (get exact windows from Researcher A).
Burst capture: 3–5 frames at slight angle variation → pick best frame by ROI
variance/least specular highlight; also enables reﬂection/moiré spoof detection
(photo-of-a-photo).
Calibration math:
1. Detect ArUco corners → locate color/grayscale patches.
2. Compute white-balance/illumination correction from the grayscale patches.
3. Apply correction to the reaction-zone pixels.
4. Convert sRGB → CIE Lab.
5. Compute Δ E2000 distance between corrected reaction color and the reagent's
documented reference color.
6. Threshold (versioned, not hardcoded permanently — see Section 6) → Positive /
Negative / Inconclusive.
Per-device-model correction proﬁle: build once against a small reference set of
phone models your team can access; apply by device ﬁngerprint at runtime.

This is an append-only local ledger — deleting, reordering, or backdating any past record
breaks every hash after it, detectable without needing to trust anyone's word.
4.3 Dual-Bound Signature
A record is only valid with both signatures present:

Stolen phone → no officer PIN. Impersonated/coerced officer without the device → no
hardware key. Breaking both simultaneously, in the ﬁeld, without leaving evidence, is a
materially higher bar than a single signature.
4.4 SMS Out-of-Band Anchor
The moment a record is chained locally, the app sends a short SMS (just the hash, ~40
characters) to a central NCB-controlled number via the device's own SIM — no gateway
subscription needed. This works even on pure 2G with no data connectivity, and creates an
independent, officer-uncontrollable timestamp witness outside the phone itself.
4.5 Offline-First Sync
Each device keeps its own sub-chain while offline — avoids merge conﬂicts between
officers who were offline in different districts simultaneously.
On reconnect: metadata+hash sync ﬁrst (tiny, works on weak signal), full image sync
when real bandwidth returns.
Storage pressure never silently discards unsynced records — it blocks new captures
and alerts instead.
Missing GPS/network → record still created, ﬂagged location_unconfirmed , never
silently blank.
4.6 Roles & Access Control
Kept deliberately light at capture, strict at the audit/export layer:
record[n].hash = SHA256( record[n].data + record[n-1].hash )

Every export embeds an imperceptible, export-speciﬁc watermark (basic LSB or
DCT-domain embedding is enough for a working demo) tying a leaked image back to
exactly who exported it, when, and why.
FLAG_SECURE  (Android) / equivalent iOS API blocks screenshots on sensitive screens.
Bulk export requires Supervisor sign-off, itself logged in the chain-hashed audit trail.
Any externally shared copy is redacted-by-default (GPS/officer ID stripped) unless
explicitly ﬂagged as a full chain-of-custody legal request.

8.2 Organizational "permissions" — the ones that actually matter for a government pilot
This is different from app permissions, and it's the part most student teams miss:
Device provisioning authority: someone (an Admin role) has to formally issue
devices + reference cards to officers, tying hardware keys to real identities. For your
SIH demo, you don't need a real provisioning bureaucracy — just show the ﬂow
(Admin screen: register device → register officer → issue card serial).
Data retention & ownership sign-off: how long does NCB expect records retained,
and who owns deletion rights? Align this to the NDPS (Seizure, Storage, Sampling and
Disposal) Rules, 2022 timelines — Researcher B should pull the exact retention
window so your app's conﬁg isn't arbitrary.
Legal basis for processing officer + location data: under India's Digital Personal
Data Protection Act, 2023, processing necessary for the prevention, detection,

11. Known Limitations (say these out loud in your deck — it builds credibility,
not weakness)
LSB watermarking is fragile to recompression — sufficient for a working demo and for
detecting naive leaks, but a determined adversary re-encoding the image can strip it.
Say this plainly if asked.
Supabase's free-tier auto-pause and Render's free-tier cold start are real constraints —
mitigate with UptimeRobot pings before your demo slot, and mention Firebase as
your always-on primary choice.
Per-device-model color correction proﬁles will only be validated against whatever
phone models your team can actually get hands on — say this is a "starter set,
extensible" rather than claiming universal device coverage.
The classiﬁer's Δ E threshold is a starting point calibrated from published reagent-
color literature, not validated against NCB's actual proprietary reagent batches — be
